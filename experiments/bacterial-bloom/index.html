Bacterial Bloom

The sibling of Cellular Erasure, run on Lenia instead of Conway's Game of Life. Three colonies now, not one: red, green and blue, each running its own growth equation, each preying on the next in a closed loop (red eats green, green eats blue, blue eats red), so the colors are real populations chasing each other, not a coat of paint. Click or drag on the dish to paint mass into whichever colony is selected in the Paint panel; turn on Evolve and the dish periodically tries a small mutation to its own growth rules, keeping it only if it produces a more textured, more alive-looking dish. The dish as a whole never settles, but a word's own small neighborhood sometimes does, and the reading below catches that: the first moment a word's spot in the dish holds still for a while, that word locks permanently, kept or blacked out depending on whether it was bright or dim right then. Paint over a locked word to contest it back open. Nothing here has to be finished for you to read it, but piece by piece, most of it eventually is.

Evolve

Every so often, try a small random mutation to the growth rules; keep it only if a short trial says the dish got more textured (measured as the field's own spatial variance) — a real, if simple, evolutionary strategy searching this dish's own rules. Off by default: it causes a brief pause every attempt, and it will drift the sliders above out of sync with each other as channels diverge.
