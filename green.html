01 — About

Designing at the
edge of what
machines imagine

Josh Urban Davis is a designer and engineer whose work sits at the intersection of machine learning, computer vision, and human-computer interaction. Based in San Francisco, he builds multimodal systems that push on questions of creativity, communication, and who gets to participate in both.

His research has received best paper awards at C&C and ASSETS, honorable mentions at CHI, and recognition from the Neukom Institute. His creative practice has been supported by a grant from the Queer Arts Council and featured in the Queer Futures Journal.

Selected work exhibited at Grey Area Foundation for the Arts, D!iverseWorks, Synaps Projects, the Blaffer Art Museum, the Chandler Center for the Arts, and at the Venice Architecture Biennale in 2021.

03 — Projects

Past & ongoing works

01

A Sudden Rush of
Blood to the Skin

Experiments in photomanipulation and shader rendering using JavaScript, 2021–2022. Source code on GitHub; more images and video on Instagram.

See more
02

if you go_say hello

A memorial: an AI trained on a deceased poet's writing, attached to a satellite, sent into orbit. Twice daily, visitors received transmissions. Over months, cosmic radiation corrupted the computer — poems degraded to noise, then silence. The collected writings were bound as a memento mori.

Venice Architecture Biennale, 2021. The poet's name remains private.

03

Hideouts

When architecture is emptied of people, it lurches towards ruin. Made during COVID lockdown — a place to hide while waiting for the world to finish ending. An exploration of tension between the physical world of viruses and the virtual world of machines.

C&C Publication
04

Postcards from the
Electric Void

GANs trained on colorized space imagery and art-historical paintings. When the machine imagines something plausible as our universe, the experience is uncanny. When it glitches, we see a recognizable fallibility — a point of empathic connection between human and machine.

NeurIPS Publication
05

Synapstraction

A brain-computer installation: EEG headsets measure your neural response to five stimuli — one per sense — each mapped to a painting implement through machine learning. Your brain serves as conduit. The senses use the artist to create an image.

Black Visual Arts Center, 2017.

06

thisObituaryDoesNotExist

StyleGAN and GPT-2 imagine photographs, death narratives, and life stories for people who never existed. Printed as funeral pamphlets indistinguishable from real ones. The website generates a new obituary on each refresh.

Visit
07

The Night Air

A collaborative poem composed by visitors during COVID-19 lockdown, inspired by miasma — the medieval belief that bad air spread plague. Ironically, COVID is airborne. Technology as connection, language as survival.

Houston Arts Alliance grant.

08

Psych-VR

VR relaxation content for astronauts in isolated spaces, developed with NASA and the Geisel School of Medicine. "Three Days and a Year On Top of a Mountain" compresses a year of timelapse footage from Gile Mountain into a single sunrise-to-sunset experience.

09

Animation

Ongoing experiments since 2017: abandoned sculptures, lab timelapse, visual programming. Each film paired with contemporary literature, soundscaped in SuperCollider.
